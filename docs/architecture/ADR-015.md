# ADR-015: Learning from your own outcomes, with statistical gates

**Status:** Accepted (Phase 9)

## Context
The app should get better at the job search from what actually happens after applying:
which jobs reply, interview and offer. A personal search produces tens or hundreds of
applications, not millions. At that size most apparent differences ("remote jobs get fewer
callbacks") are chance, and a naive analysis will confidently report noise. Advice built on
noise is worse than none, and anything invented is unacceptable.

## Decision
- **Outcomes are recorded by the user** as interview events: recruiter response, recruiter
  and phone screens, technical, hiring manager, onsite and final rounds (scheduled,
  completed, cancelled, no-show), offer, rejection, withdrawal. The application's
  milestones (`responded_at`, `first_interview_at`, `offer_at`, `rejected_at`,
  `withdrawn_at`) and status are always **derived** from those events, so edits and deletes
  stay consistent. Package generation/approval and submission come from their own rows; all
  of it forms the outcome timeline.
- **One dataset, one record per job you built a package for**, read from your rows only:
  the score and matched/missing skills at the time (package snapshot), cover letter and
  tailoring method, the resume version it was tailored from, platform, the industry you set
  (never inferred), how you applied, and the milestones. The only derived label is the job
  family, a fixed rule over the title.
- **Rates come with 95 % Wilson intervals.** Definitions: response = any employer reply
  (including rejections); interview = a screen or round scheduled or held; offer.
- **An insight is stated only when it holds up**: each group has at least 5 submitted
  applications, the outcome happened at least 3 times and failed at least 3 times overall,
  the difference is at least 10 points and 1.5x, and Fisher's exact test, Holm-corrected
  over every comparison in its family, gives p <= 0.10. The text is a template filled with
  the counts, the test and the number of comparisons. Below 10 submitted applications only
  plain facts (medians, counts) are shown. Score accuracy is the ROC AUC with a
  Mann-Whitney test and a minimum score spread; otherwise "no clear relationship yet".
- **Recommendations** come from those insights (score threshold, job families, industries,
  boards, workplace, cover letters, resume variants, skills that correlate with interviews)
  and from plain counts (skills the jobs you applied to keep asking for that your profile
  lacks, phrased "if you have it, add it"; outcomes not recorded; interviews still marked
  scheduled). Each links to where to act. **Nothing is changed automatically**; the user can
  dismiss a recommendation or mark it done.
- **The Learning Agent** runs daily and on demand, computes metrics, insights and
  recommendations, and stores a snapshot (`learning_snapshots`) only when its inputs
  changed, giving a trend of real changes. Pages compute live from current data.
- No autonomous submission, no change to the approval requirement (ADR-014).

## Consequences
- With little data the pages say "not enough data yet" more than they say anything else.
  That is intended.
- p <= 0.10 after correction is lenient for science and strict for a personal dashboard;
  every insight shows its confidence (high/medium/low) and numbers so the reader can judge.
- Outcomes not recorded look like silence; the recommendations push to record them.
- Correlation is not causation: insights describe your history, they do not prove why.
